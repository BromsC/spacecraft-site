Sabrina Gschwandtner (USA) is a visual artist and filmmaker. Two works by the artist are presented for the festival titled, “Hands at Work Video” (2017) and “Hand-painted Serpentine Dance" (2021). In the videos, archival footage is shaped, repeated and merged with colorful patterns creating a visual montage reminiscent of quilts.

Narratives of feminism, and women’s contributions to culture and industry are themes the artist explores in her works.

“Hands at Work Video” is a triangle quilt that narrates the work of women from the 20th century employed in the textile industry. Through the quilt’s patterns we see historical textile documentary films showing the skillful hands of women who weave, sew, braid and knit. As the artist explains, “…the piece ruminates on the roles of materiality and tactility in a technologically mediated future.”

The video “Hand-painted Serpentine Dance" is inspired by the French filmmaker Alice Guy-Blanché’s pioneering 1897 film of Loie Fuller’s “Serpentine Dance” performed by Mrs. Bob Walter. For the work, the artist digitally painted each frame of the video and then pieced the footage into a quilt using Adobe Premiere Pro.
