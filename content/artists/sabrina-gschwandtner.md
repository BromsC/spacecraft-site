Sabrina Gschwandtner (USA) is a visual artist and filmmaker. Two works by the artist are presented for the festival titled, “Hands at Work Video” (2017) and “Hand-painted Serpentine Dance" (2021). In the videos, archival footage is shaped, repeated and merged with colorful patterns creating a visual montage reminiscent of quilts. Narratives of feminism, and women’s contributions to culture and industry are themes the artist explores in her works.

### About the works:

“Hand-painted Serpentine Dance”, 2021. 4k video, color, silent, 1:12 mins (loops).  Sabrina Gschwandtner uses Alice Guy-Blaché’s 1897 film of Mrs. Bob Walter performing Loie Fuller’s Serpentine Dance to address the double erasure of Guy-Blaché, an under-recognized filmmaker, and Fuller, whose dance could not be copyrighted and was widely imitated. Gschwandtner’s video quilt uses diagonal seams to amplify the organic forms of the dancer’s billowing fabric, while digitally coloring her dress frame by frame in reference to early hand-colored films.

“Hands at Work Video”, 2017. HD video, color, silent, 2:42 mins (loops).  This video connects formal histories of abstraction and women’s work with textiles to digital technology. For this piece, the artist transferred historical textile documentary films to video and edited footage of handcraft, film leader and film credits into 35 layers that form a triangle quilt in motion. She edited the video like it was a digital patchwork quilt: first she chose an overall pattern design, then she made each triangle one at a time, and then she put the triangles together into sequences, thinking about content, color, rhythm, shape, and positive/negative space. For the triangles containing film footage, Gschwandtner chose clips of women’s hands at work on a textile project. The digital piece ruminates on materiality, tactility, pleasure, labor, and credit.

### About the artist:

Sabrina Gschwandtner is a visual artist and filmmaker whose work has been exhibited and screened internationally at institutions including the Victoria & Albert Museum, Walker Art Center, Museum of Arts and Design, Los Angeles County Museum of Art (LACMA), Skirball Cultural Center, Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art, Smithsonian American Art Museum, and Brooklyn Academy of Music. Her work is held in the permanent collections of LA County Museum of Art (LACMA), the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, the RISD Museum, the Walker Art Center, and other public and private collections worldwide, and has been featured and reviewed in the New York Times, Artforum, Frieze, Art in America, Los Angeles Times, Washington Post, Der Standard, Dagens Nyheter, and Svenska Dagbladet, as well as on NPR and Bloomberg News. She received a BA with honors in art/semiotics from Brown University and an MFA in film/video from Bard College. She lives in California.
